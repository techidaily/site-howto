## Part 1: Understanding Bricked ZTE Blade A73 5G

Have you ever heard the term "bricked" concerning your ZTE Blade A73 5G device? This section explains it to you in simple terms. Imagine your device becoming like a brick, unresponsive and seemingly lifeless. That's what happens when a **ZTE Blade A73 5G gets bricked**. There are two main types of brick: soft brick and hard brick.

### Soft Brick

Soft brick is like a temporary setback for your device. To see if your **ZTE Blade A73 5G is soft - bricked**, check out the details below:

- It happens when the software encounters issues, but the hardware is usually fine.
- Your device might not start properly, [<u>freeze</u>](https://drfone.wondershare.com/iphone-problems/fix-iphone-freezing-after-updating-to-ios-17.html), or [<u>get stuck on the logo screen</u>](https://drfone.wondershare.com/iphone-recovery/iphone-stuck-at-the-apple-logo.html).

Now, this is a more severe situation. In this case, your **bricked ZTE Blade A73 5G** might fall under the following conditions:

- Thesoftware and hardware have significant problems, making the ZTE Blade A73 5G device unresponsive.

### Common Causes of a Brick ZTE Blade A73 5G

Now, let's discuss why devices end up in this bricked state. It can happen due to a few common reasons:

- **Failed Updates**

Something might go wrong during the process when you're trying to update your ZTE Blade A73 5G device. This is properly because of a poor internet connection or other interruptions. As a result, your device might get confused and end up bricked.

- **Custom ROM Installations**

A custom ROM is like a customized version of your device's software. While it can bring cool new features, it can also cause issues if not installed correctly. Incompatible or poorly made custom ROMs can sometimes lead to a bricked device.

- **Unauthorized Modifications**

If you're trying to tweak your device's software without proper knowledge, you might make changes that the ZTE Blade A73 5G device can't handle. This can lead to instability and, you guessed it, a bricked device.
